# Medical Image Encryption

With the growing significance of using medical images to diagnose diseases, it has become crucial to protect these images during transfer over networks.
Unauthorized use or manipulation of medical imaging data can have detrimental effects, including invasions of privacy and wrong diagnoses that may put patients' lives in danger.
Thus, for a variety of medical images, we provide a novel image encryption algorithm. Our algorithm primarily uses the confusion and diffusion process.
A novel image- splitting technique is used in the confusion process, which is then followed by multi-layered scrambling techniques and customized patterns such as spiral zigzag, 180-degree rotational changes, and block-level permutations.
Our algorithm's central idea is to create a secretive key by using a chaotic logistic map that carefully considers the characteristics of the original image.
Using this key, the diffusion process modifies the pixel values, making the image unreadable without the right authorization.
Systematic security assessments reveal that our suggested encryption methodology performs far better than current methods in terms of key sensitivity and resistance against statistical threats.
It also excels at reducing pixel correlation, which helps it withstand differential attacks.
Our contribution is essential to the safe transmission and archiving of medical images, with the goal of promoting patient confidentiality and confidence in tele-medicine uses.
